A podcast launch fails long before anyone hits "publish." It fails in the planning, or more precisely, in the absence of planning. Here's the checklist I'd walk any new host through, in order.

**Before you record anything:**

1. **Pick a name that isn't already taken.** Sounds like a detail? It's the difference between getting found and competing with a TV show that happens to share your name. Search Spotify, Apple, and Google before you fall in love with anything.
2. **Claim a clear niche.** "Business podcast" is fighting ten thousand other shows. "The show where founders over forty talk about second acts" is a search result someone can actually find.
3. **Design cover art that works at tiny size.** On a phone it's about an inch across. Bold, readable, one clear concept, no fine print.
4. **Write your show description with keywords** a real person would type, not a vague mission statement that means nothing to a stranger.
5. **Set up proper audio.** An interface, a good mic, headphones, a quiet room. A $100 mic in a bad room sounds worse than a decent mic in a good one, so the room matters more than the gear.

**The structural decisions:**

6. **Pick an episode length and format** and commit to it for at least your first ten episodes. Changing it every week prevents you from ever building a habit.
7. **Decide your publishing cadence** and make it one you can actually keep. Weekly for a full year beats daily for a month before you burn out.
8. **Plan your first ten episodes** before you launch. This is the single biggest anti-burnout move there is, because it means you never have to stare at a blank page and wonder what to record.
9. **Choose a podcast host** (the transit and analytics platform) early, since that's where your RSS feed lives. It's a decision you really don't want to change later.
10. **Set up distribution** to Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube, and wherever your audience actually spends time.

**The pieces everyone forgets:**

11. **Test your recording workflow.** Record a three-minute clip, edit it, publish it to a private feed. Fix all the problems now, not live on the first real episode.
12. **Write a proper set of show notes** (see our guide on that) with timestamps and links. This is a recurring commitment, so get the template right early.
13. **Get a landing page or show page**, not just an app listing. This is where you send people, and where Google actually finds you.
14. **Batch a couple of episodes in advance** so a busy week doesn't break your schedule. Buffer is what protects a cadence.
15. **Book your first three guests** before you announce. Empty promises about upcoming guests are how you lose early momentum and trust.

**The honest part:** most of this is one-time overhead. Get it right in week one, and the rest of your year is just publishing. Get it wrong, and you'll be retrofitting the whole thing three months in, usually in the middle of trying to keep a weekly cadence alive.
