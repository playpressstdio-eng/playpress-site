Show notes are the unloved stepchild of podcasting. They get the last ten minutes of a busy week, a quick copy-paste of the episode title, and then everyone moves on to the next thing.

But they're one of the only assets your podcast produces that does actual search engine work. A listener who discovers you on Apple Podcasts might never visit your site. A person searching Google for a topic your guest just covered, though? That's exactly who good show notes are for.

**What a genuinely useful set of show notes includes:**

- **A two-to-three sentence summary** of who this conversation is for and what they'll get out of it.
- **Timestamped key moments**, not just a list of topics. "14:20, why most founders overengineer their first launch" is vastly better than "Launch talk," because it tells someone exactly what they'll find at that minute.
- **The guest's proper name and credentials**, spelled correctly, with the company they work for. This is how you get found when someone searches their name.
- **Links to anything mentioned**, the book, the tool, the article, the offer. Every one is a chance to be the source someone clicks.
- **A short bio of the guest**, so a stranger understands who they were listening to.

**Where most podcasts go wrong:**

1. **They write for the algorithm, not a person.** Keyword-stuffed sentences that nobody would ever actually read.
2. **They skip timestamps entirely.** The single most useful element for a busy listener, and the easiest one to leave out.
3. **They copy the description from the podcast host** and paste it everywhere, so it's generic enough to mean nothing about any specific episode.
4. **They never link out.** Every resource you don't link is a potential Google result you're simply forfeiting to someone else.

**Why it's worth the effort:**

Good show notes are real work. Listening for the moments worth timestamping, hunting down the exact resource links, and writing a summary that isn't filler, that's maybe forty minutes per episode if you're doing it right.

That time adds up. But here's the payoff: show notes keep working long after the episode publishes. Other social posts fade in a day. A set of indexed, linked show notes can bring in listeners for months, because search traffic doesn't expire the way a newsfeed does.
